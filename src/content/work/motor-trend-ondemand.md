## The challenge

Motor Trend had a strong brand and a passionate car-enthusiast audience, but no direct-to-consumer subscription business. The executive team needed proof of demand quickly, without a large up-front platform investment.

## What I did

**Shipped a 90-day MVP.** We launched the beta in July 2015 on Kaltura's turnkey video platform, across desktop and mobile web, iOS, Android, Apple TV and Roku. The trade-offs needed to hit 90 days were agreed with the executive team in advance.

**Faced the problems honestly.** The MVP proved demand and exposed its limits. Video took more than 10 seconds to start, customers couldn't subscribe through in-app purchase, the CRM was hard for operations teams to use, and the templated apps didn't fit our use cases. Our March 2016 NPS was -31. Subscribers still reached 10K on the strength of the audience.

**Ran a 45-day build-vs-buy review.** Component by component, with the architecture leads, vendors and customer references.

**Rebuilt in four and a half months.** We bought best-of-breed services where they fit (Kaltura for video, Zuora for subscription billing, Akamai for delivery, AWS for hosting) and built what made the product different: cross-device entitlements and an orchestration layer we called Halogen. Version 2.0 launched over Memorial Day weekend 2016, with paying subscribers on the service and no downtime.

**Kept improving.** Product managers and designers ran multivariate tests in Optimizely and tracked engagement in Adobe Analytics, and we added Fire TV, Chromecast and Xbox apps.

## Results

- **0 to 200K+ subscribers in 18 months**, with $10M+ in new revenue
- **NPS rose from -31 to +8** within months of the rebuild, **then to +19**
- **Monthly churn fell from 16% to 9%**
- **15K+ new subscribers a month** after version 2.0
- **Video started in about two seconds**, down from more than ten
- **A catalyst for Discovery's majority investment** in Motor Trend Group in September 2017. Motor Trend OnDemand became Discovery's first direct-to-consumer business in North America.

## What it shows

Launch the smallest product that proves demand, be clear about its compromises, and plan the rebuild before the MVP ships. The team that did this was about ten people.
